    \begin{block}{\fontsize{42}{48}\selectfont\bfseries  Our Methods and Solution}


    Our solution method is to combine a PQ-secure signature scheme with a PQ-secure aggregation scheme. We have two options in mind:

    \begin{table}[h!]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{@{}llcr@{}}
    \toprule
    & \textbf{PQ-Secure Signatures} &+ &\textbf{PQ-Secure Aggregation}\\
    \midrule
    1) & XMSS Signatures &  + & Recursive SNARKs\\ 
    2) & Lattice-based Signatures &  + & Lattice-based Folding \\
    \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{table}

        This semester, we implemented and benchmarked Option~1, and explored approaches to achieve Option~2 through research.

    Below we present the methods we have used to implement Option~1.

    \subtitleformat{SNARK.} A cryptographic proof system that allows one party (the prover) to convince another (the verifier) that they know a valid witness for a statement, without revealing it. SNARKs are widely used in zero-knowledge proofs and blockchain protocols due to their efficiency.

    
\begin{figure}
\centering
\begin{minipage}[h!]{0.38\linewidth}
\raggedright
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{\textcolor{blue}{S}}\textbf{uccinct}: Proofs are very short and quick to verify.
    \item \textbf{\textcolor{blue}{N}}\textbf{on-interactive}: No interaction needed between prover and verifier.
    \item \textbf{\textcolor{blue}{AR}}\textbf{gument of}: Strong guarantee of correctness.
    \item \textbf{\textcolor{blue}{K}}\textbf{nowledge}: The prover must actually know the witness (not just that one exists).
\end{itemize}

\end{minipage}
\hfill
\begin{minipage}[h!]{0.6\linewidth}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{snark.png}
\caption{Prover tries to prove that she knows the valid witness that satisfies the public circuit without revealing it.}
\end{minipage}
\end{figure}

    \subtitleformat{Recursive SNARK.} A recursive SNARK is a cryptographic proof system that can efficiently \textbf{verify another SNARK proof inside itself}. This allows multiple proofs to be merged together into a single, constant-sized proof.
    
    Without recursion, adding new signatures would require reprocessing all previous ones from scratch!

    \begin{figure}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=1\linewidth]{snark_aggregation.png}
        \caption{Using Recursive SNARKs to Aggregate Signatures in Multiple Levels.}
        \label{fig:enter-label}
    \end{figure}


\begin{figure}
\centering
\begin{minipage}[t!]{0.48\linewidth}
\raggedright
Recursive SNARKs allow us to merge proofs (aggregate signatures), enabling \textbf{multi-level aggregation}. This approach distributes the aggregation workload across the network, allowing it to be performed in parallel.

 Using multi-level aggregation gives us an exponential speed-up:
\vspace{1ex}

\[
T_{a} = T_{c} + \frac{T_{p} \log_{2}(S)}{k}
\]

\vspace{1ex}

\small Given $T_{c}$ as the signature-to-proof conversion time and $T_{p}$ as the time to merge $k$ proofs, then the total aggregation $T_{a}$ time for $S$ signatures is described above, \textit{assuming negligible network delay}.





\end{minipage}
\hfill

    \caption{Multi-level aggregation distributes proof merging across the network.}
\end{figure}
\end{minipage}
\end{figure}

\begin{block}{\fontsize{42}{48}\selectfont\bfseries Our Implementation}

    We implemented our system using \textbf{RISC0}, a post-quantum secure zero-knowledge virtual machine (zkVM). In a zkVM, the \textbf{guest program} is the computation whose correctness the prover attests to — it acts as the public circuit $\textcolor{blue}{f}$.
\begin{figure}
\centering
\begin{minipage}[h!]{0.38\linewidth}
\raggedright
 In our case, this guest program performs the aggregation logic. Specifically, it:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Verifies the validity of a newly submitted signature.
    \item Checks whether the signature belongs to a registered voter.
    \item Verifies each existing proof (previously aggregated signature).
    \item If all checks pass, merges their associated bitfields, indicating which voters have participated.
\end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\hfill
\begin{minipage}[h!]{0.60\linewidth}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{guest program.png}
\caption{The guest program designed for recursive SNARKs, aggregates and verifies voter signatures.}
\end{minipage}
\end{figure}

    
    Finally, the prover generates a SNARK proof that the guest program executed correctly and that the new aggregate signature is valid.



\end{block}



    \begin{block}{\fontsize{42}{48}\selectfont\bfseries Results}

    We have measured performance metrics for our solution and compared it with other work.

    Our solution introduces a multi-level aggregation approach that improves scalability, setting it apart from \textbf{KCL22} \cite{Khaburzaniya2022}, which offers only one-level aggregation and relies on one-time signatures, providing a partial solution.

    In contrast to \textbf{AAB24} \cite{LaBRADOR-Falcon}, we successfully tested our method at scale with a high number of signatures. However, the proving time and signature size in our implementation still present opportunities for further optimization.
